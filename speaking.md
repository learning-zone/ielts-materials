# IELTS General Training Speaking

## Overview

The IELTS Speaking test is a face-to-face interview with an examiner lasting **11–14 minutes**. It is divided into three parts:

| Part | Format | Duration |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Part 1** | Introduction & general questions on familiar topics | 4–5 min |
| **Part 2** | Individual long turn (cue card + preparation) | 3–4 min |
| **Part 3** | Two-way discussion on abstract topics | 4–5 min |

---

## Part 1 – Introduction & Interview Questions (2015–2025)

### Topic: Work / Study

**2025**
1. Do you work or are you a student?
2. What do you enjoy most about your job/studies?
3. Would you like to change your job/field of study in the future?

**2024**
1. What subject do you find most interesting in your studies?
2. Do you prefer working alone or in a team? Why?
3. What skills do you think are important for your job?

**2023**
1. How long have you been doing your current job?
2. What are the challenges you face at work or in your studies?
3. Do you think your job/study will change in the future?

**2022**
1. What are your plans after you finish your studies?
2. Is your workplace/school located far from your home?
3. What do you like least about your job/studies?

**2021**
1. What kind of work environment do you prefer?
2. Do you think the subjects you study are relevant to real life?
3. What motivated you to choose your current job or field of study?

## Useful Vocabulary for IELTS Speaking

### Discourse Markers
- **Adding ideas:** Furthermore, In addition, Moreover, What\'s more
- **Contrasting:** However, On the other hand, Nevertheless, Despite this
- **Giving examples:** For instance, For example, Such as, A case in point is
- **Cause & Effect:** As a result, Consequently, This leads to, Due to

### Hedging Language
- I tend to think that…
- It seems to me that…
- From my perspective…
- In my opinion / In my view…
- To the best of my knowledge…

### Expressing Agreement / Disagreement
- I completely agree with the idea that…
- While I see your point, I believe…
- That is a valid argument, however…
- I am not entirely convinced that…

### Topic-Specific Vocabulary

| Topic | Key Words |
|-------|-----------|
| Environment | sustainability, carbon footprint, renewable energy, biodiversity, deforestation |
| Education | curriculum, pedagogy, vocational training, critical thinking, academic achievement |
| Technology | artificial intelligence, automation, digital literacy, cybersecurity, innovation |
| Health | wellbeing, sedentary lifestyle, preventative medicine, mental health, obesity |
| Society | globalisation, multiculturalism, social mobility, inequality, urbanisation |

## IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors

| Band | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **9** | Expert: speaks fluently, accurately, and appropriately with no hesitation |
| **8** | Very good: fluent with only occasional lapses; uses a wide range of vocabulary |
| **7** | Good: speaks at length with some hesitation; mostly accurate grammar and vocabulary |
| **6** | Competent: communicates effectively but makes errors; some repetition |
| **5** | Modest: limited range of vocabulary; frequent errors but ideas are conveyed |
| **4** | Limited: short responses; basic vocabulary; frequent misunderstandings |

## Tips for IELTS Speaking Success

1. **Extend your answers** – Never give one-word answers; always explain, describe, or give an example.
2. **Use a range of tenses** – Demonstrate control of past, present, and future forms.
3. **Vary your vocabulary** – Avoid repeating the same words; use synonyms and paraphrases.
4. **Speak naturally** – Aim for fluency; it is better to self-correct than to stop completely.
5. **Practice with a timer** – For Part 2, practise speaking for 1–2 minutes on a cue card topic.
6. **Listen carefully** – Make sure you answer exactly what is asked, especially in Part 3.
7. **Stay calm** – If you don\'t understand a question, ask the examiner to repeat or clarify.
