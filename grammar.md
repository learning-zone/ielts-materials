## Tenses – All 12 Forms

### Simple Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Simple Present | Subject + V1 (s/es) | She **writes** every day. |
| Simple Past | Subject + V2 | They **visited** Paris last year. |
| Simple Future | Subject + will + V1 | He **will attend** the conference. |

### Continuous (Progressive) Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + V-ing | She **is studying** for the exam. |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | They **were discussing** the report. |
| Future Continuous | will be + V-ing | I **will be travelling** next week. |

### Perfect Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Present Perfect | have/has + V3 | She **has completed** the task. |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | By noon, they **had finished** the work. |
| Future Perfect | will have + V3 | By 2030, scientists **will have discovered** a cure. |

### Perfect Continuous Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Present Perfect Continuous | have/has been + V-ing | He **has been working** here for five years. |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + V-ing | She **had been waiting** for two hours before the bus arrived. |
| Future Perfect Continuous | will have been + V-ing | By July, I **will have been living** here for a decade. |

**Tips for Band 9.0:**
- Vary tenses naturally within your writing and speaking.
- Use perfect tenses to show cause-and-effect relationships across time.
- Avoid tense shifts without logical reason.

## Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with the **subject** in number and person, not with intervening words.

**Core Rules:**

| Rule | Incorrect | Correct |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Singular subject → singular verb | The list of items **are** ready. | The list of items **is** ready. |
| Plural subject → plural verb | The students **studies** hard. | The students **study** hard. |
| Collective nouns (singular meaning) | The team **are** well-prepared. | The team **is** well-prepared. |
| Collective nouns (individual actions) | The jury **has** disagreed. | The jury **have** disagreed. |
| Either/or, Neither/nor — verb agrees with the nearer subject | Neither the teachers nor the student **are** wrong. | Neither the teachers nor the student **is** wrong. |
| Indefinite pronouns (everyone, nobody, each) → singular | Everyone **have** a ticket. | Everyone **has** a ticket. |
| Subjects joined by *and* → plural | Bread and butter **are** my breakfast. | Bread and butter **is** my breakfast. (fixed phrase) |

**Tricky Cases:**
- *The number of* + plural noun → **singular verb**: *The number of applicants **is** rising.*
- *A number of* + plural noun → **plural verb**: *A number of students **are** absent.*
- Gerund as subject → **singular verb**: *Swimming **is** good for health.*

## Articles (a / an / the)

### Indefinite Articles – *a / an*

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Before a singular countable noun (first mention) | She bought **a** car. |
| *a* before consonant sounds | **a** university, **a** one-way street |
| *an* before vowel sounds | **an** hour, **an** honest man |
| Professions/roles | He is **an** engineer. |

### Definite Article – *the*

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Specific or previously mentioned noun | I bought a book. **The** book is fascinating. |
| Unique nouns | **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** government |
| Superlatives | She is **the** best candidate. |
| Geographical names (rivers, mountain ranges, oceans) | **the** Nile, **the** Alps, **the** Pacific |
| Nationalities used as groups | **The** British love tea. |

### Zero Article (no article)

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Plural/uncountable nouns in general statements | **Water** is essential. / **Dogs** are loyal. |
| Proper nouns (most countries, cities, people) | She lives in **France**. |
| Abstract nouns in general | **Happiness** is a choice. |
| Languages, sports, meals | She speaks **Spanish**. / He plays **football**. |

## Prepositions

### Time Prepositions

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **at** | specific times, night, festivals | at 9 o'clock, at night, at Christmas |
| **on** | days, dates | on Monday, on 5 July |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, longer periods | in June, in 2024, in winter |
| **by** | deadline (not later than) | Submit **by** Friday. |
| **until/till** | duration up to a point | She worked **until** midnight. |
| **since** | starting point (with perfect tenses) | He has lived here **since** 2015. |
| **for** | duration of time | I have studied for three hours. |
| **during** | throughout a period | She slept **during** the lecture. |

### Place Prepositions

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| **at** | specific point/address | at the bus stop, at 10 Baker Street |
| **in** | enclosed space, city, country | in the room, in London, in Japan |
| **on** | surface, transport (bus/train/plane) | on the table, on the bus |
| **above/below** | relative vertical position | The sign is **above** the door. |
| **between** | two items | between the bank and the post office |
| **among** | more than two items | She sat **among** the crowd. |

### Common Prepositional Phrases for IELTS Writing

- *in terms of* – In terms of cost, the second option is better.
- *with regard to / with respect to* – With regard to health, diet is crucial.
- *as a result of* – As a result of urbanisation, pollution has increased.
- *in the context of* – In the context of globalisation, cultural exchange is vital.
- *in contrast to / in comparison with* – In contrast to rural areas, cities offer more opportunities.
- *in spite of / despite* – Despite the challenges, the project succeeded.

## Modal Verbs

Modal verbs express degrees of certainty, ability, obligation, and permission.

### Modals Overview

| Modal | Use | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| **can** | present ability; informal permission | She **can** speak four languages. |
| **could** | past ability; polite request; possibility | He **could** run faster in his youth. |
| **may** | formal permission; possibility | You **may** leave early. |
| **might** | weak possibility | It **might** rain later. |
| **will** | future certainty; willingness | She **will** submit the report tomorrow. |
| **would** | hypothetical; polite request | I **would** appreciate your help. |
| **shall** | formal offer/suggestion (British English) | **Shall** we begin? |
| **should** | advice; expectation | You **should** revise your draft. |
| **ought to** | moral obligation | We **ought to** protect the environment. |
| **must** | strong obligation; logical deduction | You **must** arrive on time. / She **must** be the manager. |
| **need not / needn\'t** | absence of obligation | You **needn\'t** bring anything. |
| **dare** | challenge; formal negatives/questions | How **dare** he say that? |

### Perfect Modals (past meaning)

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| should have + V3 | criticism of past action | You **should have studied** harder. |
| could have + V3 | past possibility not taken | She **could have applied** for the job. |
| might have + V3 | past speculation | He **might have missed** the train. |
| must have + V3 | past logical deduction | They **must have left** already. |
| can\'t have + V3 | impossibility in the past | She **can\'t have finished** so quickly. |
| would have + V3 | conditional past result | I **would have helped** if you had asked. |

## Passive Voice

**Structure:** `be (conjugated) + past participle (V3)`

### Passive Across Tenses

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Simple Present | They build houses. | Houses **are built**. |
| Simple Past | She wrote the report. | The report **was written**. |
| Present Perfect | They have solved the problem. | The problem **has been solved**. |
| Past Perfect | He had submitted the form. | The form **had been submitted**. |
| Simple Future | They will announce results. | Results **will be announced**. |
| Future Perfect | They will have completed the project. | The project **will have been completed**. |
| Present Continuous | They are investigating the case. | The case **is being investigated**. |
| Past Continuous | Workers were repairing the road. | The road **was being repaired**. |
| Modal | The council should improve transport. | Transport **should be improved**. |

### When to Use Passive (IELTS Writing Task 1 & 2)

- When the agent (doer) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.
- In formal/academic writing to achieve an objective tone.
- To vary sentence structure for Band 9 range.

**Examples:**
- *The data **was collected** over a six-month period.*
- *The findings **are discussed** in the following section.*
- *Pollution levels **have been recorded** at an all-time high.*

## Conditional Sentences

### Four Main Types

| Type | Condition | Structure | Use | Example |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----|---------|
| **Zero** | Always true (fact/habit) | If + Simple Present, Simple Present | Scientific facts, general truths | If you **heat** water to 100°C, it **boils**. |
| **First** | Real/likely future | If + Simple Present, will + V1 | Possible future conditions | If she **studies** hard, she **will pass**. |
| **Second** | Unreal/unlikely present or future | If + Simple Past, would + V1 | Hypothetical situations | If I **were** the president, I **would** reduce taxes. |
| **Third** | Unreal past | If + Past Perfect, would have + V3 | Regrets about the past | If he **had applied**, he **would have got** the job. |
| **Mixed** | Unreal past condition → present result | If + Past Perfect, would + V1 | Past cause, present effect | If she **had taken** the medicine, she **would be** healthy now. |

### Unless, Provided that, As long as

- *Unless* = if not: *Unless you study, you won\'t succeed.*
- *Provided that / As long as* = on the condition that: *You can leave early, **provided that** you finish your work.*
- *Were it not for* (formal inversion): *Were it not for her support, the project would have failed.*
- *Had it not been for* (formal): *Had it not been for the delay, we would have arrived on time.*

## Reported Speech

### Tense Backshift Table

| Direct Speech | Reported Speech |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Simple Present | Simple Past |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
| Simple Past | Past Perfect |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | had to |
| shall | should |

### Reporting Verbs

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| Neutral | said, told, stated, mentioned |
| Formal/Academic | reported, claimed, argued, asserted, maintained, noted, observed, suggested, indicated |
| Negative/Critical | denied, refused, complained, objected |
| Positive | agreed, confirmed, admitted, acknowledged |

**Examples:**

- Direct: *"The results are conclusive,"* he said.
- Reported: He **stated that** the results **were** conclusive.

- Direct: *"We have finished the research,"* she said.
- Reported: She **reported that** they **had finished** the research.

- Direct: *"Please submit by Monday,"* the manager said.
- Reported: The manager **asked** them **to submit** by Monday.

### Changes in Pronouns and Time/Place References

| Direct | Reported |
|--------|----------|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the day before |
| tomorrow | the following day |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |

## Relative Clauses

### Defining vs. Non-Defining

| Type | Description | Commas | Omit relative pronoun? | Example |
|------|-------------|--------|------------------------|---------|
| **Defining** | Essential information; identifies the noun | No commas | Yes (object pronoun) | The book **(that) I borrowed** was fascinating. |
| **Non-Defining** | Extra information; can be removed | Commas required | No | London, **which is the capital of the UK**, is very cosmopolitan. |

### Relative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **who** | People (subject) | The doctor **who treated** me was excellent. |
| **whom** | People (object/formal) | The candidate **whom** they selected was brilliant. |
| **which** | Things/animals | The report, **which was published** last year, is now outdated. |
| **that** | People/things (defining only) | The car **that** he drives is electric. |
| **whose** | Possession | The student **whose** essay won the prize was delighted. |
| **where** | Places | The village **where** I grew up has changed dramatically. |
| **when** | Times | The decade **when** technology advanced most was the 1990s. |
| **why** | Reasons | That is the reason **why** she resigned. |

### Reduced Relative Clauses (Participial Phrases)

- Full: The students **who are sitting** in the front row are engaged.
- Reduced: The students **sitting** in the front row are engaged.

- Full: The bridge **that was built** in 1890 collapsed last year.
- Reduced: The bridge **built** in 1890 collapsed last year.

## Gerunds and Infinitives

### Verbs Followed by Gerund (V-ing)

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| avoid | She **avoids eating** junk food. |
| consider | They **considered moving** abroad. |
| enjoy | He **enjoys reading** academic papers. |
| finish | They **finished analysing** the data. |
| suggest | She **suggested reducing** carbon emissions. |
| deny | He **denied taking** the documents. |
| admit | She **admitted making** an error. |
| recommend | Experts **recommend exercising** regularly. |
| practise | He **practises speaking** daily. |

### Verbs Followed by Infinitive (to + V1)

| Verb | Example |
|------|---------|
| decide | They **decided to expand** operations. |
| hope | She **hopes to achieve** a band 9. |
| agree | He **agreed to sign** the contract. |
| refuse | The government **refused to act**. |
| plan | They **plan to introduce** new policies. |
| manage | She **managed to complete** the task on time. |
| expect | Researchers **expect to find** a solution. |
| tend | Prices **tend to rise** in winter. |

### Verbs Followed by Either (with change in meaning)

| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|------|--------|------------|
| **remember** | He **remembered locking** the door. (recalls past action) | Remember **to lock** the door. (instruction for future) |
| **forget** | I'll never **forget visiting** Rome. (memory of past) | Don\'t **forget to submit** your assignment. (future duty) |
| **stop** | She **stopped smoking**. (quit the habit) | He **stopped to ask** for directions. (paused in order to) |
| **try** | **Try adding** more salt. (experiment) | **Try to finish** by noon. (make effort) |
| **regret** | I **regret saying** that. (wish I hadn\'t) | We **regret to inform** you. (formal bad news) |

## Comparatives and Superlatives

### Formation Rules

| Adjective Type | Comparative | Superlative | Example |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| One syllable | add *-er* | add *-est* | tall → **taller** → **tallest** |
| One syllable ending in -e | add *-r* | add *-st* | wide → **wider** → **widest** |
| One syllable CVC | double consonant | double consonant | big → **bigger** → **biggest** |
| Two-syllable ending -y | change -y to *-ier* | change -y to *-iest* | happy → **happier** → **happiest** |
| Two/three+ syllables | more + adjective | most + adjective | important → **more important** → **most important** |
| Irregular | special forms | special forms | good → **better** → **best** |

### Irregular Forms

| Adjective/Adverb | Comparative | Superlative |
|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| good / well | better | best |
| bad / badly | worse | worst |
| many / much | more | most |
| little | less | least |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

### Advanced Comparative Structures (Band 8-9)

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| the + comparative, the + comparative | **The more** you practise, **the better** you become. |
| as + adjective + as | The results are **as significant as** previously expected. |
| not as/so + adj + as | This method is **not as effective as** the alternative. |
| Comparative + and + comparative | Temperatures are **getting hotter and hotter**. |
| no + comparative + than | It is **no better than** before. |
| far/much/even + comparative | This approach is **far more efficient** than the last. |

## Sentence Structure

### Four Sentence Types

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Simple** | One independent clause | Urbanisation is increasing rapidly. |
| **Compound** | Two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) | The population grew, **but** infrastructure did not keep pace. |
| **Complex** | One independent + one/more dependent clauses | **Although** technology has advanced, many rural areas remain underdeveloped. |
| **Compound-Complex** | Two independent clauses + one/more dependent clauses | **While** emissions rose in some sectors, renewable energy expanded significantly, **and** overall carbon output declined. |

### Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

**F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o

### Subordinating Conjunctions

| Function | Conjunctions |
|----------|-------------|
| Concession | although, even though, whereas, while, despite the fact that |
| Reason | because, since, as, given that, due to the fact that |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, as long as, in the event that |
| Time | when, while, after, before, once, by the time, as soon as |
| Purpose | so that, in order that, so as to |
| Result | so … that, such … that |

### Sentence Variety Tips for Band 9

- Mix simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences.
- Open sentences with adverbial phrases: *In recent decades, …*
- Use fronted participial phrases: *Having analysed the data, researchers concluded that …*
- Use cleft sentences for emphasis: *It is the lack of funding that hinders progress.*

## Inversion

Inversion (reversing subject and auxiliary) adds **formality and emphasis** — a hallmark of Band 8-9 writing and speaking.

### Negative Adverbials (Inverted Word Order)

| Expression | Example |
|------------|---------|
| Never | **Never have** I seen such dedication. |
| Rarely | **Rarely does** the government act swiftly. |
| Seldom | **Seldom is** such talent found. |
| Hardly … when | **Hardly had** she arrived **when** the meeting began. |
| No sooner … than | **No sooner had** the results been published **than** the debate began. |
| Not only … but also | **Not only does** this benefit the economy, but it also improves quality of life. |
| Not until | **Not until** the 20th century **did** women gain equal voting rights. |
| Only then | The situation improved. **Only then did** the government intervene. |
| Under no circumstances | **Under no circumstances should** students plagiarise. |
| In no way | **In no way does** this justify the action. |
| At no time | **At no time was** the public informed. |
| Little | **Little did** they know what lay ahead. |

### Conditional Inversion (Formal Alternative to *if*)

| Standard | Inverted (Formal) |
|----------|-------------------|
| If I were in your position, … | **Were I** in your position, … |
| If you should need help, … | **Should you** need help, … |
| If we had known, … | **Had we** known, … |

## Cleft Sentences

Cleft sentences split a simple sentence into two clauses to **emphasise** a specific element. They are highly valued at Band 8-9.

### *It-cleft* Structure: `It is/was + [emphasis] + that/who`

| Original | Cleft |
|----------|-------|
| John solved the problem. | **It was John who** solved the problem. |
| Deforestation causes habitat loss. | **It is deforestation that** causes habitat loss. |
| She failed the exam because of poor preparation. | **It was poor preparation that** caused her to fail. |

### *What-cleft* (Pseudo-cleft): `What + clause + is/was + [emphasis]`

| Original | Cleft |
|----------|-------|
| We need better policies. | **What we need is** better policies. |
| The company lacks investment. | **What the company lacks is** investment. |
| They want faster internet. | **What they want is** faster internet. |

### *All-cleft*: `All + subject + verb + is/was`

- *All I am saying is that we need to act now.*
- *All the data suggests is a moderate improvement.*

## Participle Clauses

Participle clauses reduce longer clauses to make writing more concise and sophisticated.

### Present Participle (-ing): simultaneous or causal actions

| Full Clause | Participle Clause |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Because she lacked funds, she could not continue her studies. | **Lacking funds**, she could not continue her studies. |
| As he walked through the city, he noticed the pollution. | **Walking through the city**, he noticed the pollution. |

### Past Participle (V3): passive meaning

| Full Clause | Participle Clause |
|-------------|-------------------|
| The policy, which was introduced in 2010, has been effective. | The policy, **introduced in 2010**, has been effective. |
| Because they were trained properly, the employees performed well. | **Properly trained**, the employees performed well. |

### Perfect Participle: action completed before main action

| Full Clause | Participle Clause |
|-------------|-------------------|
| After he had considered all options, he made a decision. | **Having considered** all options, he made a decision. |

## Nominalization

Nominalization converts verbs or adjectives into nouns — a key feature of **formal academic writing** (Writing Task 2, Band 8-9).

### Common Patterns

| Verb/Adjective | Noun | Example sentence |
|----------------|------|-----------------|
| develop | development | The **development** of AI is accelerating. |
| govern | governance | Good **governance** is essential. |
| pollute | pollution | Air **pollution** is a serious concern. |
| educate | education | Access to **education** reduces inequality. |
| investigate | investigation | The **investigation** revealed new data. |
| implement | implementation | The **implementation** of this policy was slow. |
| significant | significance | The **significance** of the findings cannot be overstated. |
| diverse | diversity | Cultural **diversity** enriches society. |
| efficient | efficiency | **Efficiency** gains are expected this year. |

### Verb → Noun Transformations

| Verb Sentence | Nominalized Sentence |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Scientists discovered that CO₂ levels are rising. | The **discovery** of rising CO₂ levels alarmed scientists. |
| The government decided to reduce subsidies. | The government\'s **decision** to reduce subsidies was controversial. |
| The economy grew rapidly. | Rapid **economic growth** was recorded. |

## Ellipsis and Substitution

These devices avoid repetition and demonstrate linguistic sophistication.

### Ellipsis (omitting words understood from context)

- She can speak Mandarin, and he can *(speak Mandarin)* too.
- I wanted to attend the conference, but was unable **to** *(attend)*.
- "Will you finish in time?" — "I hope **so**."

### Substitution with *so* and *not*

| Expression | Example |
|------------|---------|
| I think so / I believe so | "Is the project on track?" "I **believe so**." |
| I hope so | "Will they approve it?" "I **hope so**." |
| I'm afraid not | "Was the deadline met?" "I'm **afraid not**." |
| I think not / I believe not | "Is this approach sustainable?" "I **think not**." |

### Substitution with *do so* / *one(s)*

- The new policy is more effective than the old **one**.
- They were told to revise their essays, and many students **did so** willingly.

|-----------------|----------|

| Nevertheless | Yet |

## Pronouns

### Types of Pronouns

| Type | Pronouns | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Personal (subject)** | I, you, he, she, it, we, they | **She** analysed the data. |
| **Personal (object)** | me, you, him, her, it, us, them | The results surprised **them**. |
| **Possessive (adjective)** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their | **Their** proposal was approved. |

## Hedging Language

Hedging is essential in **academic writing** (IELTS Writing Task 2) to qualify claims and avoid overgeneralisation. Band 9 writers use hedging precisely.

### Hedging Verbs

| Strong claim | Hedged version |
|--------------|----------------|
| X causes Y. | X **appears to cause** Y. / X **may contribute to** Y. |
| It is true that … | It **seems** / **tends to be** the case that … |
| People believe that … | It **is widely believed** that … |
| Research shows that … | Research **suggests** / **indicates** that … |

### Modal Hedges

| Modal | Degree of certainty |
|-------|---------------------|
| will | Strong prediction |
| should / ought to | Expectation |
| would | Hypothetical |
| may | Moderate possibility |
| might / could | Weaker possibility |

### Adverbial Hedges

| Expression | Use |
|------------|-----|
| arguably | *This is **arguably** the most pressing challenge.* |
| apparently | *The trend is **apparently** accelerating.* |
| generally | ***Generally**, urban areas experience higher pollution.* |
| largely | *This is **largely** attributable to industrialisation.* |
| relatively | *The results are **relatively** consistent.* |
| to some extent | ***To some extent**, poverty is a global issue.* |
| in many cases | ***In many cases**, early intervention is effective.* |
| it is worth noting that | ***It is worth noting that** exceptions exist.* |

## Punctuation Rules

### Comma (,)

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Before coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses | She studied hard**, but** she did not pass. |
| After introductory adverbial phrase or clause | **Despite the evidence,** the policy was unchanged. |
| Around non-defining relative clauses | London**, which is the capital,** is very large. |
| Separating items in a list | The report covers health**,** education**,** and the economy. |
| Around parenthetical expressions | The study**, however,** was inconclusive. |

### Semicolon (;)

- Joins two closely related independent clauses (without a conjunction):
  - *The economy grew rapidly**;** unemployment, however, remained high.*
- Separates items in a complex list:
  - *Participants came from London, England**;** Paris, France**;** and Berlin, Germany.*

### Colon (:)

- Introduces a list, explanation, or elaboration:
  - *There are three main causes: poverty, poor governance, and lack of education.*
  - *The result was clear: investment had failed.*

### Apostrophe (')

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| Possession (singular) | the student**\'s** essay |
| Possession (plural ending in -s) | the students**'** essays |
| Contraction | it**\'s** = it is ; don**\'t** = do not |
| **NOT** for possessive pronoun *its* | The country lost **its** identity. *(no apostrophe)* |

### Hyphen (-) vs. En dash (–) vs. Em dash (—)

| Symbol | Use | Example |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Hyphen (-) | Compound adjectives before noun | a **well-known** author |
| En dash (–) | Range of values | pages 10–25 |
| Em dash (—) | Parenthetical comment (strong) | The result—as expected—was disappointing. |

## Noun Phrases and Determiners

### Building Complex Noun Phrases

A sophisticated noun phrase = `(Pre-modifier) + Head Noun + (Post-modifier)`

| Component | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Determiner + Adjective + Head noun | *the **significant** economic **impact*** |
| Head noun + Prepositional phrase | *the **impact of globalisation*** |
| Head noun + Relative clause | *the **factors that influence** economic growth* |
| Head noun + Participial phrase | *the **policy introduced** last year* |
| Head noun + Infinitive phrase | *the **need to address** climate change* |
| Full noun phrase | *the **significant long-term impact of unregulated industrial expansion** on biodiversity* |

### Determiners

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| Articles | a, an, the |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| Quantifiers | some, any, much, many, few, little, several, all, both, either, neither, each, every |
| Numbers | one, two, first, second |
| Predeterminers | all (of), both (of), half (of), double |

**Countable vs. Uncountable:**

| Countable | Uncountable |
|-----------|-------------|
| **many** people | **much** evidence |
| **few** students (negative) | **little** progress (negative) |
| **a few** chances (some positive) | **a little** time (some positive) |
| **a number of** issues | **an amount of** data |
| **fewer** problems | **less** pollution |

## Adverbial Clauses

Adverbial clauses modify verbs and answer **when, why, how, under what condition, despite what**.

### Types of Adverbial Clauses

| Type | Conjunctions | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Time** | when, while, after, before, once, as soon as, by the time, whenever | **Once the data had been analysed,** the team published the results. |
| **Reason** | because, since, as, given that | **Since renewable energy is cost-effective,** many countries are investing in it. |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided, as long as | **Unless emissions are reduced,** temperatures will continue to rise. |
| **Concession** | although, even though, whereas, while, despite the fact that | **Even though unemployment fell,** inequality worsened. |
| **Purpose** | so that, in order that, for fear that | The bill was amended **so that** all citizens could benefit. |
| **Result** | so … that, such … that | There was **such rapid growth that** infrastructure could not keep pace. |
| **Comparison** | as … as, than, just as | Emissions are higher **than** expected. |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though | The model performed **as if** it had been trained on larger data. |

## Parallel Structure

Parallel structure (parallelism) means using the **same grammatical form** for coordinated elements. It creates balance and clarity — essential for Band 8-9.

### Rules

1. Coordinate the **same grammatical categories**:
   - Nouns with nouns, verbs with verbs, clauses with clauses.

2. Parallel items joined by: *and, or, but, not only … but also, either … or, neither … nor, both … and*

### Examples

| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| She likes to run, swimming, and to cycle. | She likes **running, swimming, and cycling**. |
| The plan is effective, affordable, and it can be implemented. | The plan is **effective, affordable, and implementable**. |
| He was praised not only for his dedication but also he was hardworking. | He was praised **not only for his dedication but also for his hard work**. |
| The policy aims to reduce costs, improving access, and it created jobs. | The policy aims **to reduce costs, to improve access, and to create jobs**. |

### Parallel Structure in Academic Writing

- *The study **examines, analyses, and evaluates** the effectiveness of the programme.*
- *Governments must invest in infrastructure, **promote** clean energy, **and** strengthen healthcare systems.*
- *The findings are both **statistically significant** and **practically meaningful**.*
