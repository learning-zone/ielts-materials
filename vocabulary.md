## 1. Environment & Climate Change

| Word / Phrase | Part of Speech | Meaning | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **biodiversity** | noun | the variety of plant and animal life in a habitat | *Deforestation dramatically reduces biodiversity in tropical regions.* |
| **carbon footprint** | noun phrase | the total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an individual or activity | *Governments must incentivise citizens to reduce their carbon footprint.* |
| **deforestation** | noun | the clearing of forests on a large scale | *Rampant deforestation is the primary driver of habitat loss.* |
| **ecosystem** | noun | a biological community of interacting organisms and their environment | *Pollution disrupts the delicate balance of the aquatic ecosystem.* |
| **emissions** | noun | gases or other substances released into the atmosphere | *Industrial emissions are a leading cause of global warming.* |
| **fossil fuels** | noun phrase | natural fuels formed from remains of living organisms (coal, oil, gas) | *The transition away from fossil fuels is both urgent and inevitable.* |
| **greenhouse effect** | noun phrase | the trapping of the sun\'s heat in the Earth\'s atmosphere | *The intensifying greenhouse effect is causing unprecedented temperature rises.* |
| **renewable energy** | noun phrase | energy from sources that are naturally replenished | *Investment in renewable energy has surged over the past decade.* |
| **sustainability** | noun | the ability to be maintained at a certain rate without depleting resources | *Urban planners must incorporate sustainability into every new development.* |
| **depletion** | noun | reduction in the quantity or quality of something | *The depletion of the ozone layer poses serious health risks.* |
| **mitigation** | noun | the action of reducing the severity of something | *Mitigation strategies for climate change require global cooperation.* |
| **ecological footprint** | noun phrase | the impact of a person or community on the environment | *Developed nations have a disproportionately large ecological footprint.* |
| **conservation** | noun | the protection of the natural environment | *Wildlife conservation programmes have helped several species recover.* |
| **contamination** | noun | the process of making a substance impure or poisonous | *Industrial waste has led to severe contamination of local waterways.* |
| **alarming rate** | noun phrase | a speed that causes concern | *Glaciers are melting at an alarming rate due to rising temperatures.* |

## 12. Linking Words & Discourse Markers (Band 9 Level)

**Introducing an argument**
- It is widely acknowledged that…
- There is compelling evidence to suggest that…
- A persuasive case can be made for…
- It stands to reason that…

**Adding information**
- Furthermore, / Moreover, / In addition, / What is more,
- Not only… but also…
- Alongside this, / Coupled with this,

**Contrasting ideas**
- Nevertheless, / Nonetheless, / Notwithstanding this,
- Conversely, / On the contrary,
- While this may be true, it is equally important to consider…
- Despite the apparent benefits, there are significant drawbacks.

**Giving examples**
- A notable illustration of this is…
- This is exemplified by…
- To illustrate, consider the case of…

**Expressing cause and effect**
- As a consequence, / Consequently, / As a result,
- This inevitably leads to…
- The ramifications of this are far-reaching.
- This can be attributed to…

**Concession**
- Admittedly, / It must be acknowledged that…
- While one can argue that…, the counter-argument is equally compelling.

**Concluding**
- In conclusion, / To sum up, / On balance,
- All things considered,
- The evidence overwhelmingly suggests that…
